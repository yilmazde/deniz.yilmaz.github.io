Deniz Yilmaz

Researcher in Clinical Neuroscience
mind, brain, body, mental health & beyond

I'm a doctoral researcher at the Department of Psychiatry and Psychotherapy, LMU Munich, and a fellow of the Max Planck School of Cognition, working at the intersection of brain, body, and self.

My research focuses on bodily self-consciousness: how our sense of self arises, and how disruptions in these processes may contribute to psychiatric conditions. Drawing on cognitive neuroscience, clinical research, and the effects of physical activity, I aim to understand individual differences and develop more targeted therapeutic approaches.

When I'm not researching, I'm an aerial artist, performing, dancing, and exploring what the body can do.

Research

My doctoral research investigates the mechanisms behind exercise-related improvements in people with schizophrenia spectrum disorders, using machine learning models to estimate brain age, track symptom and cognitive improvements, and examine functional connectivity responses to physical activity.
